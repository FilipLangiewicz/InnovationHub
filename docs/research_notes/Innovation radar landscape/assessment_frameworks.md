# Frameworks, criteria and scoring methods to assess innovations and recommend their next path

Scope: methods to score "hidden" innovations from schools, universities, JST (jednostki samorządu terytorialnego) and other public institutions. The notes cover readiness scales, the EU Innovation Radar, public-sector and social-innovation frameworks, scaling and transfer frameworks, funder criteria, paths and instruments, transparent scoring and KPIs. The last section gives a draft rubric and a draft decision logic.

## 1. Readiness scales (TRL, MRL, CRL/CRI, KTH IRL, SRL, IRL) and their fit for non-tech innovations

### Takeaway
TRL is the common language of Polish and EU funders, and a Polish legal text (Dz.U. 2020 poz. 1495) defines nine levels. TRL does not fit organisational, educational or social innovations. For those, the Innovation Fund Denmark Societal Readiness Level (SRL 1-9) is the best published equivalent. The KTH Innovation Readiness Level (six dimensions, each 1-9) is the best model for a multi-dimension profile, but its licence status is not clear.

### Cited Findings

**TRL (EU definition)**
- Horizon 2020 and Horizon Europe use nine TRLs: TRL 1 basic principles observed; TRL 2 technology concept formulated; TRL 3 experimental proof of concept; TRL 4 technology validated in lab; TRL 5 technology validated in relevant environment; TRL 6 technology demonstrated in relevant environment; TRL 7 system prototype demonstration in operational environment; TRL 8 system complete and qualified; TRL 9 actual system proven in operational environment. TRL 5 and TRL 6 say "industrially relevant environment in the case of key enabling technologies". **[UNVERIFIED]** — [H2020 Work Programme Annex G](https://ec.europa.eu/research/participants/data/ref/h2020/wp/2014_2015/annexes/h2020-wp1415-annex-g-trl_en.pdf); [Horizon Europe General Annexes, Annex B](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/horizon/wp-call/2023-2024/wp-13-general-annexes_horizon-2023-2024_en.pdf)
- NASA made the TRL scale; Mankins (1995) gives the classic nine-level description. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Mankins 1995, Technology Readiness Levels white paper](https://aiaa.kavi.com/apps/group_public/download.php/2212/TRLs_MankinsPaper_1995.pdf)

**TRL (Polish legal definition, "poziomy gotowości technologicznej")**
- The regulation "Rozporządzenie Ministra Nauki i Szkolnictwa Wyższego z dnia 28 sierpnia 2020 r. w sprawie zadań Narodowego Centrum Badań i Rozwoju związanych z realizacją badań naukowych lub prac rozwojowych na rzecz obronności i bezpieczeństwa państwa" (Dz.U. 2020 poz. 1495) has an annex with levels I-IX. § 4 ust. 5: "Poziomy gotowości technologicznej, o których mowa w ust. 2 pkt 4, określa załącznik do rozporządzenia." — [ELI / Sejm API, Dz.U. 2020 poz. 1495](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2020/1495/text.html)
- Opening text of each level in the annex: I "obserwacja i opis podstawowych zasad związanych z funkcjonowaniem danej technologii"; III "weryfikacja koncepcji potwierdzająca analitycznie i eksperymentalnie krytyczne funkcje lub charakterystyki technologii"; IV "weryfikacja komponentów technologii w warunkach laboratoryjnych"; V "weryfikacja komponentów technologii w środowisku zbliżonym do rzeczywistego"; VI "demonstracja technologii w warunkach zbliżonych do rzeczywistych"; VII "demonstracja prototypu technologii w warunkach operacyjnych"; VIII "skompletowanie i sprawdzenie rozwijanej technologii w wyniku testów i demonstracji"; IX "sprawdzenie rozwijanej technologii w środowisku operacyjnym". — [Dz.U. 2020 poz. 1495](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2020/1495/text.html)
- The scope of this act is defence and security R&D at NCBR. Thus it is a legal TRL text, but not a general definition for all programmes. Development work in defence calls needs a technology on at least level VI. — [Dz.U. 2020 poz. 1495 (search summary)](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2020/1495/ogl/pol/pdf); the older act [Dz.U. 2011 nr 18 poz. 91](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2011/91/ogl/pol/pdf) had the same rule (research before development if level VI is not reached).
- Polish technology transfer centres (CTT) publish TRL annexes for their calls, often together with BRL. Example: "Dziewięciostopniowa klasyfikacja gotowości wdrożeniowej TRL oraz BRL" (Politechnika Śląska, 2026). — [Politechnika Śląska CITT annex](https://www.polsl.pl/rjo4-citt/wp-content/uploads/sites/747/2026/02/Zal.-nr-1-Dziewieciostopniowa-klasyfikacja-gotowosci-wdrozeniowej-TRL-oraz-BRL.pdf) (title only seen); PARP also uses a TRL annex in call rules — [PARP call annex 5](https://en.parp.gov.pl/storage/grants/documents/61/za.-nr-5-do-regulaminu-konkursu_20200226.pdf) (title only seen).

**KTH Innovation Readiness Level (KTH IRL)**
- KTH IRL has six dimensions, each on a scale 1-9: CRL (Customer), TRL (Technology), BRL (Business model), IPRL (IPR), TMRL (Team), FRL (Funding). — [DTU page on KTH IRL](https://www.inside.dtu.dk/en/research/commercialisation-and-patenting/patents-and-software/kth-innovation-readiness-level); [KTH article](https://www.kth.se/en/om/innovation/2.25073/innovationsmodell-med-global-impact-1.1414316)
- NASA TRL inspired the model. KTH uses six areas since 2013. — [KTH news](https://www.kth.se/aktuellt/nyheter/model-for-launching-innovations-generates-worldwide-demand-1.973752)
- Headline levels (KTH IRL PDF, read in this session):
  - CRL: 1 "Hypothesizing on possible needs in market"; 2 "Identified specific needs in market"; 3 "First market feedback established"; 4 "Confirmed problem/needs from several customers or users"; 5 "Established interest for product and relations with target customers"; 6 "Benefits of the product confirmed through partnerships or first customer testing"; 7 "Customers in extended product testing or first test sales"; 8 "First products sold and increased structured sales efforts"; 9 "Widespread product sales that scale".
  - CRL 3 detail: contacts with several customers, "typically ... 5-10 in B2B ... in B2C higher e.g. 10-20".
  - TMRL: 1 a few necessary competencies present; 2 "A champion is present"; 3 initial founding team; 4 complementary, diverse, committed team; ... 6 "Management and CEO in place"; 9 high-performing team maintained over time.
  - BRL: 1 "First possible business concept described (e.g. NABC)"; 3 first full business model canvas with revenues/costs; 5 "Full business model incl. pricing verified on customers (by test sales)"; 7 "Product/market fit and customers payment willingness demonstrated"; 9 business model scales with recurring revenues.
  - IPRL: 1 "Identified different forms of possible IPR that you have. Ownership is clarified"; 3 "Confirmed if protection possible and for what"; 4 "Filed first complete patent application"; 6 "IPR/patent strategy implemented"; 9 "Strong IPR support and protection for business".
  - FRL: 1 "Description of business concept (e.g. NABC)"; 2 "First small soft funding secured"; 5 "Applied for and secured additional larger funding (soft or other)"; 9 "Investment obtained".
  — [KTH Innovation Readiness Level compiled PDF (Energimyndigheten copy)](https://www.energimyndigheten.se/49cd71/globalassets/utlysningar/iaif/bilaga--kth-innovation-readiness-level_compiled_c.pdf)

**Societal Readiness Level (Innovation Fund Denmark)**
- SRL assesses "the level of societal adaptation of, for instance, a particular social project, a technology, a product, a process, an intervention, or an innovation (whether social or technical)". "SRL 1 is the lowest and SRL 9 is the highest level." — [IFD SRL definition (PDF copy, Univ. Mainz)](https://www.blogs.uni-mainz.de/research/files/2019/03/societal_readiness_levels_-_srl.pdf)
- Levels: SRL 1 identifying problem and identifying societal readiness; SRL 2 formulation of problem, proposed solution(s), potential impact, expected societal readiness, relevant stakeholders identified; SRL 3 initial testing of proposed solution(s) together with relevant stakeholders; SRL 4 problem validated through pilot testing in relevant environment; SRL 5 proposed solution(s) validated, now by relevant stakeholders in the area; SRL 6 solution(s) demonstrated in relevant environment and in cooperation with relevant stakeholders to gain initial feedback on potential impact; SRL 7 refinement and, if needed, retesting; SRL 8 proposed solution(s) and a plan for societal adaptation complete and qualified; SRL 9 actual project solution(s) proven in relevant environment. — [IFD SRL PDF](https://www.blogs.uni-mainz.de/research/files/2019/03/societal_readiness_levels_-_srl.pdf)
- IFD rule: "the lower the societal adaptation is, the better the plan for transition must be". — [IFD SRL PDF](https://www.blogs.uni-mainz.de/research/files/2019/03/societal_readiness_levels_-_srl.pdf)
- Aarhus University reports an SRL pilot in Horizon Europe (title of news item only). — [AU news: "Societal Readiness Level approach – New pilot in Horizon Europe"](https://medarbejdere.au.dk/en/administration/research-support-and-collaboration/research-support/news-from-au-forskning/show/artikel/societal-readiness-level-approach-ny-pilot-i-horizon-europe)
- A 2026 Aalborg University paper builds a Mission Readiness Level (MiREL) framework on the IFD scale (title only seen). — [Munk et al. 2026, MiREL](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/829818006/Munk_et_al._2026_MiREL_Framework_for_Mission_Assessment.pdf)

**CRI (ARENA), MRL, Investment Readiness Level**
- ARENA (Australian Renewable Energy Agency) made the Commercial Readiness Index. It gives one overall rating 1-6 of the market position of a technology. CRI 1 is "Hypothetical commercial proposition". — [Wikipedia: Commercial readiness index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_readiness_index) (secondary source)
- CRI 2-6, from prior knowledge: 2 commercial trial, small scale; 3 commercial scale-up; 4 multiple commercial applications; 5 market competition drives widespread deployment; 6 "bankable" grade asset class. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Wikipedia: Commercial readiness index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_readiness_index)
- The US DoE has an Adoption Readiness Level (ARL) framework and a CARAT tool for commercial adoption risk. — [US DoE ARL](https://www.energy.gov/technologytransitions/who-should-use-arl); [DoE CARAT PDF](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2023-03/Commercial%20Adoption%20Readiness%20Assessment%20Tool%20%28CARAT%29_030323.pdf) (titles only seen)
- MRL (Manufacturing Readiness Level) is a US DoD scale with 10 levels for production maturity. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [DoD MRL Deskbook site](https://www.dodmrl.org/)
- Investment Readiness Level (Steve Blank, 2013) is a 1-9 scale for lean start-up evidence (customer discovery, product-market fit). **[UNVERIFIED]** — [steveblank.com](https://steveblank.com/)

### Inferences
- Fit for non-tech innovations:
  - TRL, MRL and CRI do not fit organisational, educational, social or process innovations. Their anchors use "technology", "prototype" and "manufacturing".
  - SRL fits all types, because IFD defines it for "a social project ... a process, an intervention". SRL is the natural maturity axis for the non-tech profile.
  - KTH CRL, TMRL and FRL anchors are generic enough for a school or a JST. A "customer" becomes an "adopter institution". IPRL becomes "rights and openness" (who owns the material, which licence).
- For the prototype: use TRL (EU / Dz.U. 2020 poz. 1495 wording) for the tech profile and SRL for the non-tech profile. Map both to 0-5 with the same rule (see section 8). Use the KTH dimensions as the model for the other criteria, but write own anchor text. The KTH text is "All rights reserved".
- A 1-9 scale is hard for a non-expert user. A 0-5 rubric with a fixed mapping from TRL/SRL keeps the official scale visible and keeps the score simple.

## 2. EU Innovation Radar methodology (Innovation Potential and Innovator Capacity)

### Takeaway
The Innovation Radar uses a questionnaire that external experts fill in during EU project reviews. It builds two composite indicators. The Innovation Potential Indicator (IPI) is an arithmetic aggregate of Innovation Readiness, Innovation Management and Market Potential. The Innovator Capacity Indicator (ICI) is an arithmetic aggregate of Innovator's Ability and Innovator's Environment. Each indicator is normalised to 0-100 as observed score divided by maximum score. "High potential" means at least one standard deviation above the mean. This is a simple, transparent and published model that a radar prototype can copy.

### Cited Findings
- The IR is a DG CONNECT / JRC initiative from August 2013. It identifies "innovations with high potential and the key innovators behind them" in FP7, CIP and Horizon 2020 projects. — [Pesole & Nepelski 2016, JRC104870, EUR 28355 EN](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- Data: external experts of DG CONNECT used the IR Questionnaire in periodic reviews of 603 projects (October 2014 to January 2016). They identified 1,128 innovations and 1,036 key organisations. A typical project produces 1.87 innovations. — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- Innovation Readiness (IRI): "technical maturity of an evolving innovation". It covers development phase (conceptualisation, experimentation, commercialisation), steps such as prototyping, demonstration, testing or feasibility study, technological resources and time to commercialisation. — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- Innovation Management (IMI): consortium commitment to bring the innovation to market. Steps: clarify ownership and IPR, prepare a business plan or market study, secure public or private capital, engage an end-user. — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- Market Potential (MPI): demand side (market size, customer base) and supply side (barriers such as regulation or IPR issues). The 2016 report focuses on the supply side "because information on markets for individual innovations is not available". — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- Innovator's Ability (IAI): number of innovations of the organisation and their potential, plus reviewer opinion on the innovator's potential and independence. Innovator's Environment (IEI): composition and activity of partners, project innovation performance, partner commitment to exploitation, presence of end-users. "The value of the ICI is an arithmetic aggregate of the values of the IAI and IEI." — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- Normalisation formula: Normalised score = (Observed score / Max score) × 100. — [JRC104870, section 2.3](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- Categories: low, medium and high potential (innovations) and low, medium and high capacity (innovators). "Innovations with high potential: Innovations with an IPI score at least one standard deviation above the average IPI score." The same rule applies to "High Capacity Innovator" and ICI. — [JRC104870, glossary and section 2.4](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- Results: mean IPI 44.97/100 (range 12.5 to 88.33). Mean MPI 60.54, mean IMI 36.04, mean IRI 38.34. Innovation management is the weakest dimension. — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- 12% of innovations are already exploited, but 28% of mature innovations are not exploited, including nearly 50% of high-potential innovations. — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- 67% of high-potential innovations have a clearly defined owner (versus 55% of all innovations with multiple owners). University-SME co-development gives a 4.8 point higher IPI than the reference group in a regression. — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- The IR web methodology page says that the IPI "is created as an arithmetic aggregate" of the three indicators, and the IPI is built with the OECD/JRC method for composite indicators (search summary; direct fetch returned HTTP 403). — [Innovation Radar methodology page](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/methodology)
- Validation: Van Roy & Nepelski (2018) found the innovation potential index statistically sound, with room for improvement in the market potential dimension. The innovator capacity index is conceptually sound but can be improved statistically. — [JRC110926 repository entry](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC110926); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ipt:iptwpa:jrc110926)
- Nepelski & Van Roy (2021) describe the innovator capacity assessment framework in the Journal of Technology Transfer. — [Springer, JTT 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10961-020-09814-5)
- The original method report is De Prato, Nepelski & Piroli (2015), EUR 27314 EN. — cited in [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)

### Inferences
- Copy these IR design elements:
  - Three potential sub-indices (readiness, management/team, market/adopter potential) plus a separate "capacity of the owner" index.
  - Equal-weight arithmetic aggregate after 0-100 normalisation. This is easy to explain to a jury.
  - Relative categories (mean ± 1 SD) only when the database is large. For a small hackathon database, use fixed absolute thresholds.
- The IR result "28% of mature innovations not exploited" is a strong argument for the PCI problem statement ("pomysły ... pozostają znane jedynie w organizacji").
- The IR evaluator is an external expert with a questionnaire. A radar for schools and JST must work with self-reports. Thus each answer needs an evidence tag and a confidence level (section 7).

- I did not see the exact IR questionnaire items, item scales and item weights. The 2015 report (EUR 27314 EN) has them. The web page returned HTTP 403.
- The IR web platform shows labels of market maturity, for example "Exploring", "Tech ready", "Business ready", "Market ready". **[UNVERIFIED]** I could not open the page to confirm.

## 3. Public-sector and social innovation frameworks (Oslo Manual, OPSI facets, MEPIN/Copenhagen Manual, Nesta Standards of Evidence, Interreg good practice, EU social innovation, Theory of Change, SROI, Polish "innowacja pedagogiczna")

### Takeaway
These frameworks give the criteria for the non-tech profile. The Oslo Manual gives types and degrees of novelty. OPSI facets classify the direction of public-sector innovation. Nesta Standards of Evidence (levels 1-5) give a ready 5-level anchor for "evidence of effect". Interreg Europe good practice criteria ("tangible and measurable results", "potential interest to other regions") give the anchors for transfer. Polish law gives a formal category "innowacja pedagogiczna" for schools.

### Cited Findings

**Oslo Manual 2018**
- Definition: "An innovation is a new or improved product or process (or combination thereof) that differs significantly from the unit's previous products or processes and that has been made available to potential users (product) or brought into use by the unit (process)." Two types: product innovation and business process innovation. Novelty levels: new to the firm, new to the market, new to the world. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [OECD/Eurostat, Oslo Manual 2018](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oslo-manual-2018_9789264304604-en.html)

**OECD OPSI innovation facets**
- Four facets: enhancement-oriented ("How might we do X better?"), mission-oriented (a "driving ambition to achieve an articulated goal"), adaptive (test new approaches "to respond to a changing operating environment"), anticipatory (engage with "significant uncertainty about not only what works, but also what is appropriate or possible"). — [OPSI: Innovation facets – different tools for different aims](https://oecd-opsi.org/blog/innovation-facets-different-tools-for-different-aims/); [OPSI facets part 6](https://oecd-opsi.org/innovation-facets-part-6-anticipatory-innovation/)
- Facet pairs give types of change: sustaining (mission + enhancement), optimising (enhancement + adaptive), disruptive (adaptive + anticipatory), transformative (mission + anticipatory). — [OPSI facets part 2](https://oecd-opsi.org/innovation-facets-part-2-how-different-types-of-innovation-lead-to-different-types-of-change/)

**MEPIN / Copenhagen Manual**
- The Nordic MEPIN project (Measuring Public Innovation in the Nordic countries) produced the "Copenhagen Manual" to measure public-sector innovation with surveys, in the style of the Oslo Manual. **[UNVERIFIED]** — no URL verified in this session.

**Nesta Standards of Evidence**
- Level 1: an account of impact, "a logical reason, or set of reasons, for why their intervention could have an impact" (often a theory of change). Level 2: data "can begin to show effect but it will not evidence direct causality". Level 3: evaluation shows the intervention causes the impact, with less impact "amongst those who don't receive the product or service". Level 4: explain why and how, with independent validation. Level 5: "manuals, systems and procedures to ensure consistent replication and positive impact". — [Nesta: Standards of Evidence](https://www.nesta.org.uk/feature/innovation-methods/standards-evidence/); [Nesta: Standards of Evidence for Impact Investing](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/standards-of-evidence-for-impact-investing/)
- Nesta made the standards in 2012 for Nesta Impact Investments, on the basis of Project Oracle standards. Early-stage innovations need levels 1-2. More advanced innovations should seek levels 3-5. — [Nesta: Standards of Evidence for Impact Investing](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/standards-of-evidence-for-impact-investing/)

**Interreg Europe good practice**
- A good practice is "an initiative related to regional development policy which has proved to be successful in a region and which is of potential interest to other regions". "Proved successful" means it "provided tangible and measurable results in achieving a specific objective". A private initiative qualifies only if it inspired public policies. Lessons from unsuccessful practices are also used. — [Interreg Europe: Good practices](http://www.interregeurope.eu/help-centre/expert-support-services/good-practices)
- Thematic Experts assess "relevance to other regions and replicability". Project web admins check information quality. Policy Officers validate the practice for the output indicator. — [Interreg Europe: Good practices](http://www.interregeurope.eu/help-centre/expert-support-services/good-practices)
- The good practice template has fields for evidence of success (measurable outputs and results) and for potential for learning or transfer (search summary). — [Interreg Europe good practice template, Training Days 2023](https://www.interregeurope.eu/sites/default/files/2023-10/TrainingDays2023_Day2_Good_practice_template_PDF.pdf)
- The Interreg PROSPERA project has a "transferability canvas" and a "Transferability Assessment Support System (TASS)" (search summary). — [PROSPERA toolkit PDF](https://ilvo.vlaanderen.be/uploads/documents/Prospera_LR.pdf)

**EU and Polish definition of social innovation (FERS / EFS+)**
- Regulation (EU) 2021/1057 (ESF+) Art. 2(8): social innovations are "activities that are social as to both their ends and their means", which "simultaneously meet social needs and create new social relationships or collaborations between public, civil society or private organisations". **[UNVERIFIED]** wording — [EUR-Lex, Regulation 2021/1057](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2021/1057/oj)
- FERS (Fundusze Europejskie dla Rozwoju Społecznego) funds "inkubatory innowacji społecznych". An incubator searches for innovators, reaches them and supports them. Support areas include pre-incubation, research and evaluation, and "określanie efektywności kosztowej innowacji społecznych". One call requires at least 5 years of incubation experience. — [FERS call rules FERS.05.01-IZ.00-006/24](https://funduszeeuropejskie.gov.pl/media/131126/Regulaminkonkursu.pdf); [FERS assessment card, call FERS.05.01.00-IZ.00-002/23](https://funduszeeuropejskie.gov.pl/media/128578/Zalacznik9b.docx)
- FERS has separate calls to scale social innovations, for example "Wygrana rodzina" (rules of December 2024, with a chapter on scaling conditions). — [FERS "Wygrana rodzina" rules](https://funduszeeuropejskie.gov.pl/media/145061/Regulamin_konkursu_Wygrana_rodzina_zmiana_23122024.pdf)
- FERS criteria use three kinds: merit criteria scored 0-1 (pass/fail), horizontal criteria, and point criteria that can be decisive. The FERS Monitoring Committee approves the criteria (search summary). — [FERS assessment card](https://funduszeeuropejskie.gov.pl/media/128578/Zalacznik9b.docx)

**Theory of Change and SROI**

## 4. Scaling and transfer frameworks (Nesta, ExpandNet/WHO, CFIR, RE-AIM, Rogers)

### Takeaway
All scaling frameworks repeat the Rogers attributes (relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability). ExpandNet/WHO packs them into the CORRECT checklist. CFIR 2.0 adds "evidence-base", "adaptability", "design" and "cost" in its Innovation domain. RE-AIM gives post-transfer outcome measures (Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance). These items give a repeatable yes/no checklist for "transferability" and give the "mierniki skuteczności" of a replication path.

### Cited Findings
- Rogers diffusion of innovations: five perceived attributes (relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability). A 2025 paper gives a measurement scale for these attributes plus reinvention. — [PMC12530589, diffusion of innovations measurement scale](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12530589/)
- ExpandNet/WHO CORRECT attributes: credible, observable, relevant, relative advantage, easy to install and understand, compatible, testable. — [RNAO guidance PDF](https://rnao.ca/media/6007/download); [PMC9338559, scalability study in Ghana, Malawi, Uganda](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9338559/)
- WHO/ExpandNet gives a scalability checklist. The assessment is the first of "nine steps" to build a scale-up strategy. ExpandNet defines scale-up as "deliberate efforts to increase the impact of successfully tested pilot projects so more people benefit and policies and programs take hold on a lasting basis" (paraphrase in source). — [PMC9338559](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9338559/)
- Relevance, ease and compatibility depend on local problems, priorities, norms and beliefs. — [PMC9338559](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9338559/)
- A review compares scalability assessment tools ("why, how and who"). — [Zamboni et al. 2019, Health Policy and Planning, PMC6788216](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6788216/)
- CFIR 2.0 (Damschroder et al. 2022, Implementation Science 17:75) has five domains: Innovation, Outer Setting, Inner Setting, Individuals, Implementation Process. It has 48 constructs and 19 subconstructs. ### Inferences
- A transferability criterion with yes/no items is more repeatable than a free 0-5 judgement. Proposed five items (score = count of "yes", 0-5):
  1. Documentation exists that another institution can follow (Nesta L5, CORRECT "easy to install").
  2. The cost to adopt is known and fits a typical school or gmina budget (CFIR "cost").
  3. No special infrastructure or rare skills are necessary (Rogers "complexity", inverse).
  4. It is compatible with the law and standard processes of the target institution type (Rogers "compatibility", CFIR "Policies & Laws").
  5. A small pilot is possible and the result is visible in a short time (Rogers "trialability", "observability").
- Use RE-AIM dimensions as the "mierniki skuteczności" of the replication path: number of adopter institutions (Adoption), people reached (Reach), effect measured (Effectiveness), fidelity (Implementation), use after 12 months (Maintenance).
- Use CFIR Outer and Inner Setting as matching data: a target institution with the same problem, budget and a "champion" is a better match.

## 7. Transparent and repeatable scoring (rubrics, MCDA, inter-rater reliability, uncertainty, bias, LLM-assisted scoring)

### Takeaway
A weighted sum of behaviourally anchored 0-5 criteria, with non-compensatory gates and an explicit evidence tag per criterion, is the most transparent model for a jury. Human expert scoring of proposals has low agreement, so a fixed rubric plus a reliability check is necessary. LLMs can extract evidence and propose scores. They show low run-to-run consistency and known biases, and agreement with humans depends on the scale (best on 0-5). Thus an LLM must only suggest scores with quoted evidence, and a rule engine plus a human must set the final score.

### Cited Findings

**Human scoring reliability**
- Reviewers of the same NIH grant applications showed very low agreement. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Pier et al. 2018, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1714379115)
- Earlier work found low-to-fair inter-rater reliability between reviewers for manuscripts and grant proposals (as summarised in a 2026 study). — [arXiv 2601.19925](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19925)
- ICC interpretation: below 0.5 poor, 0.5-0.75 moderate, 0.75-0.9 good, above 0.9 excellent. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Koo & Li 2016, J Chiropr Med](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/)
- Krippendorff's alpha: α ≥ 0.800 for reliable data, α ≥ 0.667 for tentative conclusions. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Krippendorff, Computing Krippendorff's Alpha-Reliability](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2021-03/Computing%20Krippendorff%27s%20Alpha-Reliability.pdf)

**MCDA**
- The IR composite indicators use a normalised arithmetic aggregate, built with the OECD/JRC composite indicator method. — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf); [IR methodology page (search summary)](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/methodology)
- The OECD/JRC Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators recommends sensitivity and uncertainty analysis of weights and aggregation. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [OECD/JRC Handbook 2008](https://www.oecd.org/sdd/42495745.pdf)
- AHP (Saaty) derives weights from pairwise comparisons. A consistency ratio below 0.10 is acceptable. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Saaty 1990, EJOR](https://doi.org/10.1016/0377-2217(90)90057-I)
- Horizon Europe uses thresholds per criterion (non-compensatory gate) plus a total; EIC uses GO/NO-GO per criterion. — see section 5 sources.

**LLM-assisted scoring**
- Grading scale: across six benchmarks, "the grading scale of 0-5 yields the strongest human-LLM alignment" (intraclass correlation). The scale choice changed agreement even when internal reliability was high. Pooled reliability hid differences between benchmarks and subgroups. — [Li et al. 2026, arXiv 2601.03444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03444)
- Run-to-run consistency: LLM ratings have low agreement over multiple runs with the same prompt, and turning off sampling hurts agreement with humans (search summary). — [arXiv 2510.27106](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.27106.pdf)
- Healthcare scoping review (13 studies): judge-expert agreement 0.66-0.96, median 0.83. The same judge fell from 0.83 (one expert) to 0.51 (majority of experts). The reference standard matters. — [arXiv 2605.25273](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.25273)
- High agreement does not prove that LLM judges rate the same constructs. Expert-LLM weighted kappa ranged 0.17-0.86 across 21 dimensions. Validation sample sizes are rarely justified. — [arXiv 2605.16354](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16354)
- PRISM benchmark: κ = 0.688 on aspect identification, weighted κ = 0.465 on grounding; within-one-point agreement 75.7-100% on ordinal tasks. — [arXiv 2605.26730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26730)
- MT-Bench study: a strong LLM judge reached over 80% agreement with human preferences, similar to human-human agreement. It showed position bias, verbosity bias and self-enhancement bias. **[UNVERIFIED]** — [Zheng et al. 2023, "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)

### Inferences
- Rubric design rules for the prototype:
  1. Use 0-5 for each criterion. The 0-5 range matches Horizon Europe and has the best published human-LLM alignment.
  2. Give each level a behavioural anchor (an observable fact, for example "need confirmed by at least 3 institutions"). Do not use adjectives only.
  3. Allow "brak danych" (unknown) as a value different from 0. Show it in the report.
  4. Store, for each criterion: score, anchor text, evidence quote or field, evidence tag, source URL, who or what set the score, and the rubric version.
  5. Use non-compensatory gates for each path, not only a total.
  6. Show a sensitivity check: does the recommended path change if a weight changes by ±5 points? If yes, show "rekomendacja niestabilna".
- Evidence tag per criterion (proposal):
  - E0 "brak danych" (no information): the criterion is unknown.
  - E1 "deklaracja" (author statement without detail).
  - E2 "deklaracja z opisem lub dokument wewnętrzny" (statement with detail, numbers, names, or an internal document).
  - E3 "źródło weryfikowalne" (public record: patent register, publication in PBN, evaluation report, Interreg or NCBR database, BIP resolution).
- Confidence level: share of total weight with tag E2 or E3. High ≥ 70%, medium 40-70%, low < 40%. Score range: compute the total with all unknown criteria at 0 (minimum) and at 5 (maximum). Show "62 (zakres 55-74), pewność: średnia".
- LLM role: extract facts from the free-text description and from public sources, propose a level with a quote, and flag missing data. Use temperature 0 and run 3 times; if the runs differ by more than 1 level, mark the criterion "do weryfikacji". A deterministic rule engine computes totals and paths. A human (PCI expert) confirms or overrides, and the system logs the override with a reason.
- Calibration: before use, two or more PCI experts score the same 10-20 sample innovations. Compute ICC or Krippendorff's alpha per criterion. Rewrite anchors of criteria with low agreement.
- Bias controls: hide the name of the author and institution during scoring (avoid halo of a famous university). Do not let text length affect the score (verbosity bias). Randomise the order of compared innovations (position bias).

### Gaps
- I did not find a study that compares LLM scores with human expert scores on innovation or grant proposals in the public sector. The cited studies cover abstracts, peer review, healthcare and general benchmarks.
- I did not read Pier et al. 2018, Koo & Li 2016 or Krippendorff in this session.

## 8. KPIs for an innovation-radar system

### Takeaway
No standard KPI set exists for an "innovation radar" in public institutions. The closest standard is the EU knowledge transfer metrics (JRC/ASTP 2020), which counts outputs such as research agreements, invention disclosures, licences and spin-offs, and says that outputs must be read with inputs. A radar needs funnel KPIs (found, assessed, matched, transferred) plus quality KPIs (rater agreement, data completeness, time to answer).

### Cited Findings
- JRC and ASTP made "Knowledge Transfer Metrics – Towards a European-wide set of harmonised indicators" (Campbell, Cavalade, Haunold, Karanikic, Piccaluga, Karlsson Dinnetz, 2020, EUR 30218 EN). The expert group reviewed the 2009 set of seven core and seven supplementary indicators for public research organisations and gave 10 recommendations. Output metrics "cannot be viewed in isolation from inputs". — [JRC120716 repository entry](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC120716); [Knowledge4Policy project page](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/projects-activities/knowledge-transfer-metrics-%E2%80%93-towards-european-wide-set-harmonized-indicators_en)
- The 2009 core set (EC expert group on knowledge transfer metrics) includes research agreements, invention disclosures, patent applications, patent grants, licences executed, licence income and spin-offs established. **[UNVERIFIED]** list — [JRC120716](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC120716)
- A follow-up JRC-ASTP phase studied composite KT indicators and a scoreboard. Critical success factors: a recognised set of harmonised indicators, annual collection in each Member State, dedicated resources. — [ASTP: composite indicators phase 2](https://astp4kt.eu/news/exploration-of-composite-indicators-for-knowledge-transfer-phase-2)
- Innovation Radar reports use counts per project (1.87 innovations per project), share exploited (12%) and share of mature but not exploited innovations (28%). — [JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- RE-AIM gives post-adoption outcomes (Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance). **[UNVERIFIED]** — [RE-AIM.org](https://re-aim.org/)

### Inferences
- Proposed radar KPIs (system level), with a suggested definition:
  - Discovery:
    1. Number of innovations recorded per month, by institution type (school, university, JST, other) and by powiat.
    2. Share of records from automatic discovery versus manual submission.
    3. Coverage: share of institutions in the region with at least one record.
  - Assessment quality:
    4. Share of records assessed within 14 days of submission.
    5. Median time from submission to first assessment.
    6. Data completeness: share of total criterion weight with evidence tag E2 or E3.
    7. Inter-rater agreement (ICC or Krippendorff's alpha) on a calibration sample; target ≥ 0.75 ICC or ≥ 0.667 alpha.
    8. Share of LLM-proposed scores that experts change by more than 1 level.
  - Matching:
    9. Time to first match (days from assessment to the first proposed partner, adopter or instrument).
    10. Match acceptance rate (share of proposed matches that both sides accept for a contact).
    11. Number of contacts made (meetings) per assessed innovation.
  - Outcomes (12-24 months, per path):
    12. Number of replications completed (another institution uses the solution).
    13. Number of licence agreements, spin-offs or spółki celowe created.
    14. Funding obtained after the recommendation (PLN), by instrument.
    15. Readiness progression: share of innovations with TRL or SRL +1 or more in 12 months.
    16. Duplicate projects avoided: number of new submissions linked to an existing similar solution instead of a new development.
  - Users:
    17. Share of authors who find the justification understandable (short survey).
    18. Usability score of the tool (for example the System Usability Scale). **[UNVERIFIED]** benchmark: a SUS score of about 68 is average — [MeasuringU: SUS](https://measuringu.com/sus/)
- Per-path "oczekiwane mierniki skuteczności" (shown with each recommendation): see section 9, table "Mierniki per path".

### Gaps
- I found no published KPI set for an innovation radar of schools or JST.
- I did not read the full JRC 2020 report, so the final 2020 core list is not confirmed.

## 9. Draft scoring rubric, decision logic and path-to-instrument mapping (synthesis for the prototype)

### Takeaway
The draft uses nine criteria on a 0-5 scale with behavioural anchors, two weight profiles (tech and non-tech), an evidence tag per criterion, non-compensatory gates and ordered path rules. Each rule cites the criterion values that trigger it, so the report can show the justification and the expected effectiveness measures. All anchors come from the frameworks above (TRL, SRL, KTH IRL, Innovation Radar, Nesta, Interreg, CORRECT/CFIR, PARP).

### Cited Findings
- Sources of each element: IR three sub-indices and 0-100 normalisation ([JRC104870](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)); KTH IRL dimensions ([DTU](https://www.inside.dtu.dk/en/research/commercialisation-and-patenting/patents-and-software/kth-innovation-readiness-level)); SRL ([IFD](https://www.blogs.uni-mainz.de/research/files/2019/03/societal_readiness_levels_-_srl.pdf)); TRL ([Dz.U. 2020 poz. 1495](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2020/1495/text.html)); Nesta levels ([Nesta](https://www.nesta.org.uk/feature/innovation-methods/standards-evidence/)); Interreg criteria ([Interreg Europe](http://www.interregeurope.eu/help-centre/expert-support-services/good-practices)); CORRECT ([PMC9338559](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9338559/)); PARP novelty minimum ([PARP Annex 2](https://en.parp.gov.pl/storage/grants/documents/62/Zalacznik-nr-2-Kryteria-oceny-merytorycznej-pomyslu-przez-Ekspertw-na-e_28042020.pdf)); 0-5 scale ([arXiv 2601.03444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03444)); partnerstwo innowacyjne condition ([lexlege.pl art. 189 Pzp](https://lexlege.pl/zamow-publ/oddzial-6-partnerstwo-innowacyjne/13953/)).

### Inferences

**A. Profile selection**
- Profile T (technologiczna): product or process with a technical core (device, software, material, method of production). Maturity axis = TRL.
- Profile N (nietechnologiczna): organisational, educational ("innowacja pedagogiczna": programowa, organizacyjna, metodyczna), social or process innovation. Maturity axis = SRL.
- Mixed case (for example an app for a gmina service): compute both profiles and show both. The path rules use criterion values, so they work for both.

**B. Criteria and 0-5 anchors**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | Potrzeba / problem | No problem stated | Problem stated by the author only | Problem described with local data or named users | Need confirmed by ≥ 3 users or institutions | Need confirmed in ≥ 2 institutions, with data on its scale | Need in a public strategy or data for many institutions, plus demand signals (requests, letters of intent) | KTH CRL 1-5; IR MPI demand side |

- Total score: S = Σ (w_i × k_i / 5), range 0-100. This copies the IR rule "observed / max × 100".
- Score range for unknown criteria: S_min with unknown = 0, S_max with unknown = 5.
- Confidence: share of weight with evidence tag E2 or E3 (high ≥ 70%, medium 40-70%, low < 40%).
- Weights are a starting point. PCI experts can set them with AHP pairwise comparison (accept CR < 0.10) and the tool shows the sensitivity of the path to ±5 points.

**D. Gates (non-compensatory, checked first)**
- G1 Need gate: K1 < 2 gives the path "Doprecyzuj problem" (clarify the problem). No other path.
- G2 Legal gate: a critical legal or ethical barrier flag (for example health data without a legal basis) gives "Konsultacja prawna przed dalszymi krokami".
- G3 Duplicate gate: K2 = 0 and the similarity search finds a more mature solution gives "Połącz z istniejącym rozwiązaniem". The report shows the linked solution.
- G4 Confidence gate: confidence "low" gives "Uzupełnij dane" plus a provisional path marked "wstępna".
