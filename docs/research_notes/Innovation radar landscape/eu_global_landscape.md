# EU and global landscape: platforms, tools and methods to find, assess, share and transfer (public-sector) innovations

Scope: outside Poland, state as of 21 November 2026. EU programmes that include Poland are in scope. Sources are linked inline. "Snippet" means that the fact comes from a search-engine summary of the page, because the page itself refused the fetch (HTTP 403). Treat snippet facts as less certain.

## Q1. Public-sector innovation observatories, case libraries and good-practice databases

### Takeaway
Europe has many public case libraries (OPSI, Interreg Europe, Erasmus+, Public Sector Tech Watch, SIM, Joinup), but almost all of them are passive repositories: manual submission, human validation, keyword search and filters, no scoring of potential and no automatic matching. The only EU system with a documented, quantitative scoring method is the EU Innovation Radar, and it covers EU-funded research projects, not schools or municipalities.

### Cited Findings

**EU Innovation Radar (DG CNECT, method by JRC)**
- DG CNECT manages the site. The JRC developed the methodology. Innovations are categorised by market maturity and disruptive potential with JRC indicator systems — [Digital Strategy: Innovation Radar](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/innovation-radar)
- The data comes from independent experts who review ongoing EU-funded projects (snippet) — [JRC114418](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC114418); [Innovation Radar FAQ](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/faq/0)
- The method uses two composite indicators: (1) Innovation Potential = Innovation Readiness + Innovation Management + Market Potential; (2) Innovator Capacity = Innovator's Ability + Innovator's Environment (the partner organisations) (snippet) — [Innovation Radar methodology](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/methodology); [Nepelski et al. 2020, J. Technology Transfer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10961-020-09814-5); [JRC104870 PDF](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC104870/jrc104870_innovation-radar_2_final.pdf)
- The platform assigns the maturity category automatically from the "Innovation Management" and "Innovation Readiness" scores (snippet) — [Innovation Radar methodology](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/methodology)
- The four maturity levels are "exploring", "tech ready", "business ready" and "market ready" — [Circular Cities and Regions: EU Innovation Radar Platform](https://circular-cities-and-regions.ec.europa.eu/support-materials/tools-and-methods/eu-innovation-radar-platform)
- A "Market Creation Potential" (MCPI) level is assigned automatically from 5 signals in the Innovation Radar data set (snippet) — [Innovation Radar FAQ 11](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/faq/11)
- A JRC statistical audit found the innovation potential index "statistically sound", with room to improve the market potential dimension. The innovator capacity index is "conceptually sound" but can be improved statistically (snippet) — [JRC107545](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC107545)
- Search and browse facets: maturity level, market needs, topic, market creation potential, women-led innovations, funding programme type, organisation type, EU country. Each innovation shows maturity, market creation potential, key innovators with location, and SDG contribution — [Circular Cities and Regions](https://circular-cities-and-regions.ec.europa.eu/support-materials/tools-and-methods/eu-innovation-radar-platform)
- The repository passed 10,000 innovations (news of 19 January 2023). The public tool launched at Digital Day 2018 and has iOS and Android apps. Ministers of 23 countries signed the Innovation Radar Declaration — [Digital Strategy: 10,000 milestone](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/innovation-radar-european-commissions-repository-innovations-market-potential-hits-10000-milestone); [Digital Strategy: Innovation Radar](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/innovation-radar)
- The Commission page was updated on 20 November 2026 but lists no change to method, prize or data access in 2024–2026 — [Digital Strategy: Innovation Radar](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/innovation-radar)
- The Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026–2027 still refers to the Innovation Radar — [HE WP 2026-2027 Annex](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/document/download/2049ffe7-b712-4da8-98a9-72b45606e72d_en)
- I found no bulk download or public API for Innovation Radar data. The JRC enriched the data with financial, patent and venture capital data in a separate study — [JRC114418](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC114418)

**OECD Observatory of Public Sector Innovation (OPSI)**
- The Case Study Platform filters: countries, level of government, sector, year of launch, stage of innovation, recognition, innovation tags. Cards show title, country, image, short description and tags. A "Submit a case" form exists. The site licence is CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO. No API or download is shown — [OPSI innovations](https://oecd-opsi.org/innovations/)
- Before its 2018 relaunch the platform had "over 400 cases". The 2018 Call for Innovations received 276 submissions from 58 countries (snippet; exact page not verified) — [OPSI Call for Innovations post](https://oecd-opsi.org/?p=1507)
- OPSI runs a yearly Call for Government Innovations. The 2026 edition is the 8th call, with a downloadable submission form — [OPSI 2026 call](https://oecd-opsi.org/blog/the-oecd-call-for-government-innovations-2026-edition/); [2026 submission form](https://oecd-opsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Submission-Form_2026-Call-for-Government-Innovations_download.docx)
- Innovation Facets model: four types — enhancement-oriented, adaptive, mission-oriented, anticipatory. OPSI argues that a portfolio is a better bet than a single project in uncertainty. OPSI warns of "mission lock-in" — [OPSI Innovation Facets brief 2021](https://oecd-opsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/OECD-Innovation-Facets-Brief-Innovation-Portfolios-2021.pdf); [Tackling Policy Challenges (2022)](https://oecd-opsi.org/publications/tackling-policy-challenges/)
- OPSI offers a free Portfolio Exploration Tool for self-assessment (snippet) — [Tackling Policy Challenges](https://oecd-opsi.org/publications/tackling-policy-challenges/)
- The Toolkit Navigator links "hundreds" of free innovation toolkits and has "over 100 reviews" from practitioners — [OPSI Toolkit Navigator](https://oecd-opsi.org/toolkit-navigator/)
- OPSI lists a Positive Deviance toolkit with principles and steps — [OPSI: Positive Deviance Initiative](https://oecd-opsi.org/toolkits/positive-deviance-initiative/)

**JRC Public Sector Tech Watch (PSTW)**

- The predecessor AI Watch list "must not be considered a complete and systematic inventory" and stopped at 31/12/2021 — [JRC Data Catalogue](https://data.jrc.ec.europa.eu/dataset/7342ea15-fd4f-4184-9603-98bd87d8239a)

**Interoperable Europe Portal (formerly Joinup)**
- Joinup now operates as the Interoperable Europe Portal. The Interoperable Europe Act entered into force in April 2024 — [Interoperable Europe: Joinup collection](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/joinup)
- The EU Open Source Solutions Catalogue launched in 2025. Its stated goal is to help public bodies avoid duplicate development. It covers 30+ public-sector domains and federates national and local catalogues with the ADMS-AP specification — [data.europa.eu: EU OSS catalogue](https://www.data.europa.eu/en/news-events/news/discover-eu-open-source-solutions-catalogue-strengthening-collaboration-across)

- On 7 August 2026 the portal added a consolidated "reuse form" to report the implementation of a solution (snippet) — [Interoperable Europe: Joinup collection](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/joinup)

**Interreg Europe Policy Learning Platform (good-practice database)**
- Definition: an initiative "which has proved to be successful in a region and which is of potential interest to other regions". Success means "tangible and measurable results in achieving a specific objective". A private initiative qualifies only with evidence that it inspired public policy. Unsuccessful practices can also be used for learning — [Interreg Europe: What is a good practice?](http://www.interregeurope.eu/help-centre/expert-support-services/good-practices)
- Validation has up to three steps: project web admin (OK / NO / MAYBE), Policy Officer, then Thematic Experts who assess relevance to other regions and replicability — [Interreg Europe: good practices](http://www.interregeurope.eu/help-centre/expert-support-services/good-practices)
- The template asks for "factual evidence" of success (measurable outputs/results) and has a separate field "Potential for learning or transfer" — [Interreg GP template 2023](https://www.interregeurope.eu/sites/default/files/2023-10/TrainingDays2023_Day2_Good_practice_template_PDF.pdf); [Interreg GP submission and validation slides](https://WWW.INTERREGEUROPE.EU/sites/default/files/2023-10/TrainingDays2023_Day2_GP_submission_and_validation.pdf)
- A common reason for rejection: "evidence of success not clearly demonstrated (no quantitative / qualitative data)" — [Interreg validation slides](https://WWW.INTERREGEUROPE.EU/sites/default/files/2023-10/TrainingDays2023_Day2_GP_submission_and_validation.pdf)
- The GovTech Lab Lithuania is itself an entry in this database — [Interreg Europe GP: GovTech Lab Lithuania](https://interregeurope.eu/good-practices/govtech-lab-lithuania)

**Erasmus+ Project Results Platform (education; includes Poland)**
- The platform marks "good practices" and "success stories": finished projects with distinguished impact, policy contribution, innovative results or creative approach. National Agencies and the Commission select them every year — [Cedefop](https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/node/26395); [EURIreland](https://eurireland.ie/erasmusplus/erasmus-project-results-platform)
- Selection uses six qualitative and quantitative criteria, among them impact (inspires other applicants) and communication (results understandable to a wide public). I did not find the full list of six — [CEUR-WS Vol-3696 paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3696/article_4.pdf)
- Filters include success stories/good practices, ongoing/completed, with results, with factsheets — [EURIreland](https://eurireland.ie/erasmusplus/erasmus-project-results-platform)
- A study found that only a small portion of projects get the good-practice label, and that applicant experience with Erasmus+ calls is a main variable of success — [CEUR-WS Vol-3696 paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3696/article_4.pdf)

**ESF+ Social Innovation+ and the Social Innovation Match (SIM) database (includes Poland)**

The initiative transfers and scales up tested solutions in employment, education, skills and social inclusion. Budget: €197 million for 2021–2027 (another source gives €198 million) — [ESF+: Competence centres](https://european-social-fund-plus.ec.europa.eu/en/competence-centres-social-innovation)

Users showcase initiatives, search for projects tested in other countries, contact organisations and find partners for transnational calls — [ESF+: Social Innovation Match](https://european-social-fund-plus.ec.europa.eu/en/social-innovation-match); [ESFA Lithuania](https://esf.lt/en/social-innovation-match-database-a-new-possibility-of-visibility-and-recognition-for-you-and-your-organization)

**UNDP Accelerator Labs: solutions mapping**
- Solutions mapping finds locally developed fixes that work and builds on them. It rests on the idea that "innovation occurs all around us", in villages and farming communities. The concept "grassroots to global" comes from Prof. Anil Gupta and the Honeybee Network — [UNDP: Grassroots to Global reckoning, part 1](https://www.undp.org/acceleratorlabs/blog/grassroots-global-reckoning-part-1); [UNDP: Helping small go big](https://www.undp.org/node/26581)
- In El Salvador, the Lab and TECHO joined a community survey tool with a solutions-mapping module. They recorded recurring behaviours and grassroots solutions, plus demographic and infrastructure data for context. They published results on Appropedia (open wiki) and an open guide to knowledge products — [UNDP: part 2](https://www.undp.org/acceleratorlabs/blog/grassroots-global-reckoning-part-2); [UNDP: part 3](https://undp.org/node/375946)

**Positive deviance**
- The approach assumes that solutions already exist in the community. The steps: find the positive deviants, identify the behaviours that cause their success, then spread them. Common frameworks: the "4Ds" and Bradley et al. — [Baxter 2022, THIS Institute](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/193204/1/Baxter%202022%20-%20THIS%20Institute%20The-positive-deviance-approach.pdf); [Deloitte Insights](https://www2.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/what-darwin-can-teach-government-harnessing-positive-deviants-to-help-solve-vexing-problems-within-your-organization.html)
- Andrews (Harvard Kennedy School) applies the idea to public-sector reform in development — [HKS: Explaining positive deviance](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/explaining-positive-deviance-public-sector-reforms-development)

### Inferences
- The EU Innovation Radar is the best documented pattern to reuse: a short expert questionnaire, composite indicators (readiness, management, market potential, innovator capacity), and an automatic maturity label with four levels. For the public sector, "market potential" can become "transfer potential" (how many similar institutions have the same problem).
- Interreg Europe gives clear, citable acceptance criteria: measurable evidence of success, relevance to other regions, replicability, and a public-policy link. These criteria map well to a transparent scoring rubric.
- OPSI facets (enhancement, adaptive, mission, anticipatory) can be a ready-made classification field. Its licence CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO means that reused OPSI case text must keep the share-alike licence.
- COI data shows that most public innovation is adoption. Thus the main value of a "radar" for schools and municipalities is to find and transfer existing solutions, not only to discover new inventions. "Spread" should be counted only when a second organisation implements the solution.
- Nesta's "hidden innovation" concept gives the theory for the PCI task: public-sector innovations do not show in patents or R&D statistics, so the tool must use other signals (project reports, local news, grants, competitions, school and municipal websites).
- SIM, EEN, EUIP and PSTW show that the EU already has partner-search databases. None of them uses AI detection or semantic matching (as far as public sources show). This is a gap the prototype can fill.
- Positive deviance suggests an "automatic detection" route: find institutions with outlier results in public statistics, then look for the practice behind the result.

## Q2. University technology transfer and matching platforms

### Takeaway
Technology-transfer matching has moved from open online marketplaces to curated, service-heavy models: universities pay a subscription, companies use the platform free, and human experts support the algorithm. The EU side changed in 2026: the Horizon Results Platform gave its market-oriented results to the new EU Innovation Platform (EUIP), launched 3–4 June 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **IN-PART**: an online partnering platform that connects industry and academia by alignment of research priorities. Algorithms match research to companies, and in-house STEM experts add to the matching — [IN-PART for universities](https://www.in-part.com/for-universities); [IN-PART blog: matchmaking](https://in-part.com/blog/university-industry-matchmaking/)
- IN-PART: 250+ universities and research institutes, 6,000+ companies. Universities pay a subscription. A third-party listing says it is free for companies and has no success fees — [IN-PART for universities](https://www.in-part.com/for-universities); [G2: IN-PART](https://www.g2.com/products/in-part-connect/discuss)
- IN-PART was acquired in March 2023 by Inova, a biopharma partnering platform from Lyon (snippet) — [Mercia](https://www.mercia.co.uk/?p=22476)
- An expert view on IN-PART-style transfer: improvement "is not about cold-calling" — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/research-intelligence/improving-universities-tech-transfer-not-about-cold-calling)
- **Wellspring Scout and Flintbox**: Scout is an AI search platform that aggregates publications, grants, patents, startups and research partners. Its base came from Covalent Data — [Wellspring Scout](https://www.wellspring.com/scout); [Wellspring about](https://www.wellspring.com/about)
- Flintbox is a marketplace for licensable university technologies. Flintbox listings also feed the AUTM Innovation Marketplace — [Wellspring Flintbox](https://wellspring.com/products/flintbox); [CASRAI guide](https://www.casrai.org/guides/tech-transfer-licensing-marketplaces); [Wellspring news](https://www.wellspring.com/news/wellspring-launches-new-flintbox-platform)
- Flintbox started in 2003 at the University of British Columbia. Sources conflict on the acquisition year (2009 or 2010) — [Research Money](https://researchmoneyinc.com/article/flintbox-becomes-us-owned-subsidiary-with-sale-to-wellspring-worldwide); [Wikipedia: Wellspring Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellspring_Worldwide)
- **yet2**: its own account says the pure online IP marketplace "would ultimately fail". Success-fee market-making was one reason. yet2 moved to a proactive, service-oriented model — [yet2: Why online IP marketplaces fail](https://www.yet2.com/?p=1221)
- An academic case study says yet2's change shows the limits of technology exchange "exclusively through internet databases" — [Inderscience case study of yet2.com](https://www.inderscience.com/filter.php?aid=44611)
- **NineSigma**: intermediaries change pricing over time. NineSigma raised the project fee and dropped the transaction fee — [Lopez & Vanhaverbeke, MPRA paper](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/27016/1/Reviewed_version_file_not_for_review_final_2_pdf)
- NineSigma clients pay per project, by a "discovery fee" (risk-reward), or by subscription — [Sramana Mitra interview with NineSigma president](https://sramanamitra.com/2011/09/30/outsourcing-matthew-heim-president-of-ninesigma-part-1/)
- **Enterprise Europe Network (EEN) Partnering Opportunities**: crawled snapshots show 5,148 and later 5,709 active profiles. Profile types: technology offer (TO), technology request (TR), business offer, business request, R&D request. IDs have the form type + country + date, for example "TOCZ20260126019". Profiles carry classification codes for technology and market — [EEN partnering opportunities (page 35)](https://int.een.ec.europa.eu/partnering-opportunities?page=35); [EEN partnering opportunities (page 13)](https://een.ec.europa.eu/partnering-opportunities?page=13)
- **EU Innovation Platform (EUIP)**: DG RTD launched it at the European Innovation Summit, Brussels, 3–4 June 2026 — [EU4Digital](https://eufordigital.eu/european-commission-launches-eu-innovation-platform/); [Public Buyers Community](https://public-buyers-community.ec.europa.eu/communities/procure-innovation-eu/news/european-commission-launches-eu-innovation-platform)
- All market-oriented results of the Horizon Results Platform (HRP) moved to EUIP in draft mode for owner review. HRP keeps only results that inform public policy, governance or societal outcomes. A June 2026 HRP newsletter was "the last" — [Commission newsletter 76566](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/informatics/newsletter-archives/76566); [HRP page](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/portal/screen/opportunities/horizon-results-platform)
- EUIP first serves EU-funded innovators. The Commission says it will open to other innovators, advisers, investors and corporates "in the coming months" — [EU4Digital](https://eufordigital.eu/european-commission-launches-eu-innovation-platform/)
- The Horizon Results Booster was relaunched as "the Booster" in November 2024 to help Horizon Europe beneficiaries bring results to use — [R&I news, 7 Nov 2024](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/news/all-research-and-innovation-news/new-booster-services-help-eu-funded-researchers-bring-their-results-use-2024-11-07_en)
- **EIC**: the EIC Corporate Partnership Programme ran over 100 matchmaking events with over 120 corporate partners and over 100 business collaborations. Corporates publish challenges as open calls on the EIC Community Platform — [EIC Scaling Club recap](https://eicscalingclub.eu/news/highlights-from-the-eic-corporate-partnership-programme-webinar-video-recording); [EIC Corporate Partnership Programme](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/business-acceleration-services/corporate-partnership-programme_en)
- EIC Business Acceleration Services report "+20,000" one-on-one meetings between EIC awardees and corporates, procurers and investors since 2021 — [EIC BAS](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/bas_en)
- I found no product called "EIC Marketplace". The closest is the EIC Community Platform — [EIC Community Platform](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-communities/eic-community-platform_en)

### Inferences
- The IN-PART model (paying supply side, free demand side, algorithm plus human curation) is the most credible business model for a regional "radar". PCI could pay as the "supply side" for the region, and adopters (schools, municipalities, companies) use it free.
- The yet2 and NineSigma history shows that a pure listing site with success fees does not sustain itself. Matching needs an active intermediary. In Podkarpackie, PCI can play the role of the intermediary; the prototype should give PCI staff a work queue, not only a public catalogue.
- The EEN profile model (offer / request type, technology and market codes, short structured profile) is a ready data model for "connect creators with users". The prototype can export a profile in the same structure as an exit route to EEN.
- EUIP (2026) and the policy-only HRP are new "exit routes" for the "recommend path" step. A good recommendation engine can name the right EU platform for each path (commercialisation → EUIP/EEN; policy result → HRP; social innovation → SIM).

### Gaps
- Not researched or no reliable data found: Inteum (TTO management software), Innoget, ASTP and AUTM programmes in detail, the AUTM Innovation Marketplace size, IN-PART pricing for universities.
- I found no public API for EEN profiles, EUIP or HRP in this research.
- I found no independent evaluation of the success rate of IN-PART or EEN matches.

## Q3. Commercial AI innovation-scouting and idea-management tools

### Takeaway
Commercial tools split into two groups: (1) internal idea-management suites (HYPE, Qmarkets, Brightidea, IdeaScale, Viima/HYPE Boards, Sideways 6) that collect ideas from staff and use similarity search to flag duplicates; (2) external scouting suites (ITONICS, Valuer, StartUs Insights, PatSnap Eureka, Dealroom, Crunchbase) that mine startup, patent and news databases. Prices are mostly hidden and quote-based; known entry points run from a free plan up to five-figure yearly contracts. None of them targets hidden innovations in schools or municipalities.

### Cited Findings

**Idea management (internal crowdsourcing)**
- **Qmarkets**: the platform highlights similar ideas during submission. The feature indexes title, description, tags and categories in SOLR. It uses keyword relevance (stemming, stop words, fuzzy matching, field weights), "not generative AI". ## Q4. Open-source alternatives and their licences

### Takeaway
The mature open-source platforms for public participation (Decidim, CONSUL Democracy, Go Vocal free edition) all use the copyleft AGPLv3 licence. I found no mature open-source tool for idea management or technology-transfer matching. The EU itself promotes reuse of open-source public solutions through the Interoperable Europe Portal.

### Cited Findings
- **Decidim**: free open-source participatory democracy platform for cities and organisations. Code under AGPL v3, content under CC BY-SA, data under the Open Database License. Used by "over 450 cities and organisations" — [Decidim FAQ](https://decidim.org/faqs); [Wikipedia: Decidim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decidim); [About Decidim](https://www.decidim.org/about/)
- **CONSUL Democracy** (from Madrid City Council): licensed AGPL; it supports participatory budgets and citizen proposals. An early fork of CONSUL powered decidim.barcelona in 2016 — [OSOR case study: CONSUL Democracy](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/inline-files/osor-case-study-of-consul-democracy.pdf); [CONSUL NL repository](https://github.com/consul-nl/consul-nl); [Wikipedia: Decidim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decidim)
- **Go Vocal (formerly CitizenLab)**: open-core model. The repository `citizenlab-oss` is the free edition under AGPLv3. The main repository is the commercial edition with a licence that does not allow self-hosting. The vendor's own page says the code "can only be used through a paid commercial license", which conflicts with the AGPL statement — [GitHub: CitizenLabDotCo/citizenlab](https://github.com/CitizenLabDotCo/citizenlab); [GitHub: citizenlab-oss](https://github.com/CitizenLabDotCo/citizenlab-oss); [Go Vocal open source page](https://govocal.com/open-source); [Go Vocal blog](https://govocal.com/blog/heres-why-we-are-going-open-source)
- **EU OSS Solutions Catalogue** (Interoperable Europe Portal, 2025): a federated catalogue of public-sector open-source solutions to avoid duplicate development — [data.europa.eu](https://www.data.europa.eu/en/news-events/news/discover-eu-open-source-solutions-catalogue-strengthening-collaboration-across)
- Open data examples: PSTW cases (JRC Data Catalogue) and the beta.gouv portfolio (data.gouv.fr) are downloadable — [JRC Data Catalogue: PSTW](https://data.jrc.ec.europa.eu/dataset/e8e7bddd-8510-4936-9fa6-7e1b399cbd92); [data.gouv.fr: beta.gouv](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/realisation-de-services-publics-numeriques-en-mode-produit-coordonnes-par-le-programme-interministeriel-beta-gouv)
- A search for open-source idea-management software found mostly general project tools and listicles, no dedicated maintained project with a confirmed licence — [CPO Club list](https://cpoclub.com/tools/best-open-source-idea-management-software/); [FitGap open source list](https://us.fitgap.com/search/idea-management-software/open-source)

### Inferences
- Decidim and CONSUL are good references for proposal workflows (submit, comment, support, evaluate, status). But they are large Ruby on Rails systems. For a 20-hour prototype it is simpler to copy their data model ideas than to deploy them.
- AGPL is copyleft. The repository rules (AGENTS.md) say: tell the team before you use a copyleft licence. Code reuse from Decidim, CONSUL or Go Vocal needs this decision. Reuse of ideas or data models does not.
- The lack of a dedicated open-source tool for public innovation scouting is a real gap. A permissively licensed (MIT or Apache 2.0) prototype supports the "transferability" criterion and the KOWR/PCI transfer of rights.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the licence of smaller tools (UseResponse, Your Priorities, Loomio) or any open-source technology-transfer software.
- I did not check whether Decidim has an existing module for innovation scoring or matching.

## Q5. Evidence and lessons: why good-practice databases fail, and what drives real transfer

### Takeaway
Evidence points to the same causes of failure: content becomes stale, few people contribute, search cannot show which entry is current or proven, and a listing alone does not cause adoption. Transfer happens through people and intermediaries: peer relationships, an active broker, referral routes, a clear "core" of the innovation, and adaptation in the new organisation.

### Cited Findings

**Why repositories fail**
- Vendor research on company knowledge bases: more than 94% of active content was untouched in a given month; 76% of registered users never created a document; cleanup work gets no reward ("Nothing launches") — [Slite: Why knowledge bases fail](https://slite.com/learn/why-knowledge-bases-fail) (vendor source; treat numbers with caution)
- Search returns matches but "cannot tell them which answer is current, approved, or safe to use" — [Slite](https://slite.com/learn/why-knowledge-bases-fail)
- Government knowledge bases waste up to 10 hours per week of a public servant's time through poor search and silos (vendor or opinion source) — [Civic Roundtable](https://civicroundtable.com/blog/governments-knowledge-problem)
- UK commentary: valuable government user research stays "buried" because of old processes — [Global Government Forum](https://www.globalgovernmentforum.com/the-uk-government-is-sitting-on-a-user-research-goldmine-so-why-isnt-it-being-used/)
- Weak records management at the source is a main cause of poor data. Controls that produce good-quality data from the start are "a far better strategy" — [World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/stories/all/open-data-information-governance-quality/)
- The AI Watch case list was "not a complete and systematic inventory" and stopped at the end of 2021 — [JRC Data Catalogue](https://data.jrc.ec.europa.eu/dataset/7342ea15-fd4f-4184-9603-98bd87d8239a)
- In Erasmus+, the good-practice label goes mostly to experienced applicants, so the label can favour experienced organisations over new ones — [CEUR-WS Vol-3696 paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3696/article_4.pdf)
- Online IP marketplaces failed as pure databases (yet2) — [yet2](https://www.yet2.com/?p=1221); [Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/filter.php?aid=44611)
- Interreg Europe rejects entries without quantitative or qualitative evidence of success — [Interreg validation slides](https://WWW.INTERREGEUROPE.EU/sites/default/files/2023-10/TrainingDays2023_Day2_GP_submission_and_validation.pdf)

**What drives transfer and replication**
- 73% of Danish public innovations are inspired by or copied from others — [Apolitical](https://apolitical.co/en/articles/denmark-releases-worlds-first-survey-government-innovation)
- COI asks whether "copying more and adapting even less" could use resources better (snippet) — [OPSI](https://oecd-opsi.org/?p=18336)
- A systematic review of 181 studies (1990–2014) gives antecedents of public-sector innovation at four levels: environment, organisation, innovation characteristics and individual. Most studies ignore the outcomes of innovation — [de Vries, Bekkers & Tummers 2016, Public Administration](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/51035)
- The same authors wrote a 2018 meta-synthesis on the diffusion and adoption of public-sector innovations (not read in full) — [de Vries, Tummers & Bekkers 2018](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/362167/De_Vries_Tummers_Bekkers_The_diffusion_and_adoption_of_public_sector_innovations_PPMG.pdf?sequence=1)
- Nesta, from 50+ funded innovations: scale needs four areas (organisational growth, licensing and affiliation, partnerships, replication through delivery networks). Innovations with clear referral routes through public services found demand more easily — [Nesta: Centre for Social Action Innovation Fund](https://nesta.org.uk/node/37012)
- Nesta "Making it big" (2014): "identifying the core" of an innovation is important; the scaler must decide how tightly to control the spread — [Nesta: Making it big](https://www.nesta.org.uk/report/making-it-big-strategies-for-scaling-social-innovations/)
- Scale happens only when the context is right; funders and public services can create this context — [Nesta: Nurturing the seeds of change](https://nesta.org.uk/report/nurturing-seeds-change)
- Public systems need intermediaries to spread new approaches: "Private markets don't assume new products and services will reach customers by themselves" — [Nesta: Four reflections on public service innovation](https://www.nesta.org.uk/blog/four-reflections-public-service-innovation/)
- Young Foundation/Nesta (2007): spread models range from tight ownership (franchise, licence) to loose spread with no owner. Tacit knowledge and key relationships matter — [In and out of sync (2007)](https://youngfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/In-and-out-of-sync-the-challenge-of-growing-social-innovations-Sept-2007.pdf%20)
- Bloomberg Cities supports transfer with blueprints, learning tours, peer exchange and implementation grants, not only a database — [Bloomberg Cities Idea Exchange](https://citiesideaexchange.bloomberg.org/?p=136)
- ESF+ Social Innovation+ pays for transnational transfer projects and runs communities of practice. A 2023 event in Paris focused on scale and transfer — [ESF+ competence centres](https://european-social-fund-plus.ec.europa.eu/en/competence-centres-social-innovation); [ESFA Lithuania: scaling the French way](https://esf.lt/en/scaling-up-social-innovations-the-french-way)
- UNDP: return findings to the community, not only extract information — [UNDP part 2](https://www.undp.org/acceleratorlabs/blog/grassroots-global-reckoning-part-2)

### Inferences

**Design rules that follow from the evidence**
- Make freshness visible: show the date of the last confirmation for each entry and ask the creator to confirm it periodically. Mark entries without evidence as "unverified".
- Lower the cost of contribution: let the system draft the structured record from a public source (web page, project report), and ask the creator only to confirm it. This answers the "76% never contribute" problem.
- Score evidence, not only novelty: use Interreg-style criteria (measurable results, transferability, public-policy link) next to Innovation-Radar-style readiness.
- Record adoption events: count a transfer only when a second organisation implements the solution (COI definition). Show "adopted by N organisations" as a key metric.
- Give the "core" and the "adaptable parts" as fields of each innovation record (Nesta "identifying the core"). This helps the adopter.
- Put a human broker (PCI staff) into the loop: a work queue for validation and introductions, as in IN-PART, EIC and Interreg.

**Cross-cutting: ideas to reuse and gaps to fill (for the prototype)**

| Item | Reuse from | Gap that the prototype can fill |
|---|---|---|
| Classification | OPSI facets; Nesta hidden innovation types | Apply to schools, universities and municipalities in Poland |
| Assessment | Innovation Radar indicators and 4 maturity levels; Interreg criteria | No public tool scores public-sector innovations with clear, explainable criteria |
| Detection | UNDP solutions mapping; positive deviance; scouting tools mine web and patents | No tool mines public-institution sources (school sites, municipal news, project results) automatically |
| Duplicate check | Qmarkets and HYPE similarity search (keyword) | Multilingual semantic similarity across Polish and EU sources (OPSI, PSTW, SIM, Erasmus+) |
| Matching | IN-PART (algorithm plus experts); EEN offer/request profiles; SIM partner search | Match an innovation to adopters with the same problem, and to EU exit routes (EUIP, EEN, SIM, HRP) |
| Business model | IN-PART (supply side pays, demand side free) | Regional agency (PCI) as paying operator and broker |
| Openness | Joinup reuse; PSTW and beta.gouv open data | Permissive licence plus open data export, unlike AGPL platforms and closed SaaS |

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous, peer-reviewed study of the usage of EU good-practice databases (visits, reuse rates). The failure evidence above is mostly from vendors and commentators.
- I could not read the OECD "Toolkit for adopting ideas from other cities" (HTTP 403). It is probably the most relevant source for transfer between public institutions.
- I found no measured adoption rate for practices in the Interreg, OPSI or SIM databases.
