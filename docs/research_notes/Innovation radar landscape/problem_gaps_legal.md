# Radar innowacji: problem evidence, gaps and legal checklist (Poland, November 2026)

Scope: (A) evidence for "hidden" public-sector innovations in Poland, (B) gaps in current solutions and possible differentiators, (C) legal constraints for the design. Date of research: 21 November 2026. The legal part is a design checklist. It is not legal advice.

Legend: [VERIFY] = the source is secondary, the sources do not agree, or the information can be old.

## A1. Problem evidence: innovation performance and diffusion in Poland and Podkarpackie

### Takeaway
Poland and Podkarpackie are both at about two thirds of the EU innovation average. Podkarpackie is weak in the transfer of knowledge into use: sales of new innovations, public-private co-publications and ICT specialists. International data show that most public-sector innovation is adaptation of solutions from other places (73% in Denmark). Thus a mechanism to find and spread existing local solutions attacks the main path of public-sector innovation.

### Inferences
- Pitch line: "Podkarpackie has strong creativity signals (design applications rank 16 of about 240 EU regions) but very weak conversion into use (sales of new innovations rank 230; public-private co-publications rank 223)". This is a "hidden innovation" signal in official EU data.
- Pitch line: "In Denmark, 73% of public-sector innovations are adapted from others. Reuse is the main channel of public-sector innovation, but Poland has no common channel for it."
- The strong 2023-2025 increase in "Innovative SMEs collaborating with others" (+53.1) suggests that the regional ecosystem is ready for matching tools.
- NIK findings on CTT and spółki celowe show that the classic research-to-business transfer channel is weak. A radar must cover non-technological and public-sector innovations, which these channels do not cover.
- GUS measures innovation only in enterprises. No Polish official statistic measures innovation in schools, JST or public institutions. This absence is itself evidence for the problem.

### Gaps
- I found no Polish study that counts duplicate development of the same IT systems or apps by different JST. NIK reports on JST IT focus on security and licences, not on duplication. A possible proxy: count similar tenders (for example "aplikacja mobilna" for gminy) in the BZP API (see B2). I did not do this count.
- I found no NIK report on good-practice sharing in JST or schools.
- I found no Polish public-sector innovation survey similar to the Danish barometer. I found no newer Danish number than the 2013-2014 survey for the "73%" figure.
- I did not read the full NIK reports; some NIK facts come from search snippets.

## B1. Gaps in current solutions (good-practice databases, tech-transfer platforms, idea tools)

### Takeaway
Current databases are mostly supply-side catalogues with manual entry and expert validation. They cover one sector or one funding source (EU research projects, Interreg projects, AI/blockchain cases). None found joins public-sector, school, university and social innovations with demand signals and follow-up after the first entry. Evaluations of their actual reuse are almost absent.

### Cited Findings
- **EC Innovation Radar**: it covers only EU-funded research projects. External experts identify innovations during project reviews. — [Innovation Radar methodology](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/methodology); [EC digital strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/innovation-radar)
- Innovation Radar has a "Market Creation Potential Indicator" (MCPI) from 5 signals, with 5 levels from "low" to "very high". About 14% of innovations have "high" or "very high" market creation potential (FAQ). The methodology page says 35% show market creation potential. [VERIFY: different thresholds] — [FAQ](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/faq/11); [methodology](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/methodology)
- Innovation Radar maturity classes: about 50% "Exploring", 20% "Tech ready", 20% "Business ready", 10% "Market ready" (methodology page). — [methodology](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/methodology)
- A JRC validation study found the Innovation Potential Indicator statistically sound, with most room for improvement in the market potential component. — [JRC121066](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC121066); [Springer article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10961-020-09814-5)
- The Innovation Radar repository passed 10,000 innovations (January 2023). It covers about one quarter of EU-funded R&I actions under Horizon 2020. — [EC news](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/innovation-radar-european-commissions-repository-innovations-market-potential-hits-10000-milestone)
- **Interreg Europe good-practice database**: a 2024 training deck gives "+3300 good practices approved by Platform experts" (older figures: ~700 in 2019, ~2,000 later). Experts validate on "relevance to other regions and replicability". Only Interreg project partners submit. [VERIFY: undated counts] — [Interreg Europe GP validation (PDF)](https://interregeurope.eu/sites/default/files/2024-09/Day%202%20-%20GP%20submission%20%26%20validation%20%282%29.pdf); [GP validation guidance note](https://interregeurope.eu/sites/default/files/2024-09/Good%20practice%20validation_Project%20guidance%20note.pdf); [Interreg in numbers 2019](https://www.interregeurope.eu/sites/default/files/2022-01/2019-04-10-interreg-in-numbers.pdf)
- **Public Sector Tech Watch (EC/JRC)**: 1,617 cases (2024 report), only emerging technologies (AI, blockchain, others). Observatory managers research the cases, or case owners report them. — [PSTW report (PDF)](https://joinup.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/document/2024-04/PSTW%20-%20AI%20and%20blockchain%20in%20the%20European%20Public%20Sector.pdf); [PSTW cases](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/public-sector-tech-watch/cases)
- **OECD OPSI** keeps a case study database and toolkits. The search found no evidence of how well these databases cause reuse. — [OPSI](https://oecd-opsi.org/?p=27846)
- **Polish social innovation incubators (POWER 2014-2020)**: 4 calls, more than PLN 114 million from the ESF. Micro-innovations were tested, and the ministry lists examples with "potential for wider use". — [Ministerstwo Funduszy i Polityki Regionalnej](https://www.gov.pl/web/fundusze-regiony/wspieramy-innowacje-spoleczne---uruchamiamy-inkubatory-innowacji-spolecznych)
- An EPALE blog says that dissemination (move from project level to system level) is "the real test" of a social innovation. [Opinion] — [EPALE](https://epale.ec.europa.eu/pl/blog/innowacje-spoleczne-w-polsce-od-pomyslu-do-zmiany)
- The ex-post evaluation of the NCBR "Innowacje Społeczne" programme (Ecorys Polska, Taylor Economics) says the programme needs continuation in a changed form, and the institutional system needs improvement. — [gov.pl attachment](https://www.gov.pl/attachment/8dfeb218-3d6e-4466-bd86-77cbc5b9159c)
- **NIK on tech transfer**: special-purpose companies were not effective (see A1). — [money.pl](https://www.money.pl/gielda/nik-spolki-celowe-maja-slabe-efekty-w-transferze-wynikow-badan-do-gospodarki-6337330316613249a.html)

### Inferences
Gaps that a hackathon prototype can address (inferred from the findings above):
1. **Sector silos.** Each database covers one source: EU research (Innovation Radar), Interreg projects, emerging-tech cases (PSTW), social incubators. No found tool covers schools + JST + universities + NGOs with one record format.
2. **Manual, expert-heavy entry.** Innovation Radar, Interreg and PSTW depend on experts or project owners. This does not scale to about 24,000 schools and JST.
3. **Technology bias in scoring.** Innovation Radar measures technical readiness and market potential. It does not fit organisational, educational or social innovations, where "transferability" and "cost to adopt" matter more than "market".
4. **Supply-side only.** The catalogues list solutions. They do not show which institutions need them now.
5. **No follow-up.** No found database records what happened after the entry (adopted elsewhere, abandoned, scaled). OPSI says diffusion must be designed into all phases.
6. **No evaluation of reuse.** I found no evaluation that measures how many database entries were reused. This is a weakness of the field and an argument for a tool that tracks reuse.

### Gaps
- I found no user-feedback study on Polish good-practice databases (for example of the Związek Miast Polskich or of ORE for schools). I could not check their size, update date or participation.
- I found no data on participation rates in public-sector idea-management platforms.
- I did not read the full POWER ex-post evaluation; I have no number of micro-innovations that were scaled after the incubators.

## B2. Differentiators, precedents and past hackathon winners

### Takeaway
Feasible and original differentiators: (1) demand-side matching from free public procurement data (BZP API); (2) explainable, signal-based scoring adapted from the EC Innovation Radar MCPI, but for non-technological innovations; (3) an "adapt, not invent" path and duplicate-work alerts, supported by the Danish 73% figure; (4) lifecycle tracking after identification, as OPSI recommends. HackNation 2025 had 16 challenges and 16 winners; the jury selected solutions with "the largest potential for deployment".

### Cited Findings
**Demand-side data**
- The BZP (Biuletyn Zamówień Publicznych) API on e-Zamówienia is free. Its terms say that no access application is necessary. Endpoint: `ezamowienia.gov.pl/mo-client-board/api/notices/`. — [UZP, Regulamin korzystania z API (PDF)](https://media.ezamowienia.gov.pl/pod/2023/01/Regulamin-korzystania-z-API.pdf)
- UZP reports use only data from the e-Zamówienia platform; EU OJ notices imported into the BZP search are not in these reports. — [UZP report data source (PDF)](https://media.ezamowienia.gov.pl/pod/2022/07/rodlo-danych-raportu-danych-ogolnych-o-zamowieniach.pdf)
- Third-party tools describe BZP notices as public information that is reusable. [VERIFY: not an official UZP statement] — [Apify actor README](https://apify.com/nebulosa/poland-bzp-tenders.md)

**Precedents**
- EC Innovation Radar: signal-based MCPI with 5 levels; maturity classes (see B1). — [methodology](https://innovation-radar.ec.europa.eu/methodology)

[VERIFY: lab counts differ between sources: 60 labs (2019-2020) vs. 92 labs in 116 countries] — [UNDP AccLabs 2020 (PDF)](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/migration/germany/undp-gro-AccLabs-2020.pdf); [UNDP blog](https://www.undp.org/blog/helping-small-go-big-mainstreaming-what-happens-margins)

## C1. GDPR / RODO (data about creators, minors, scraping, DPIA)

### Takeaway
Store the minimum personal data, prefer data about the innovation over data about the person, and use consent or public task as the legal basis by design. In Poland the age for consent of a child to information society services is 16, so 16-17-year-old creators can give consent themselves, but other limits apply. If the system collects personal data from public web pages, the Bisnode case shows that the Article 14 information duty applies and that courts read its exceptions narrowly.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR Art. 8: a child of 16 years or more can give consent for information society services offered directly to the child. Below 16, a parent must give or authorise consent. Member States can lower the age to 13. — [GDPR Art. 8 (gdpr-text.com)](https://gdpr-text.com/pl/read/article-8/)
- Poland kept 16 years: the first draft of the Polish data protection act had 13 years, but after the consultations the limit stayed at 16. [VERIFY: secondary sources from 2018] — [podatki.biz](https://www.podatki.biz/artykuly/rodo-w-praktyce-zbieranie-zgody-od-dzieci_34_38423.htm); [money.pl (older draft with 13 years)](https://www.money.pl/gospodarka/wiadomosci/artykul/zgoda-na-przetwarzanie-danych-osobowych,53,0,2404149.html)
- Art. 8 GDPR does not change other age rules in national law. — [gazetaprawna.pl](https://www.gazetaprawna.pl/twoje-prawo/artykuly/10867398,jak-zmieni-sie-ochrona-dzieci-w-internecie-po-wejsciu-w-zycie-rodo.html)
- The EDPB published a statement on age assurance (11 February 2025). — [EDPB (PL, PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-07/edpb_statement_20250211ageassurance_pl.pdf)
- **Bisnode case (scraped/registry data)**: in March 2019 the UODO President fined Bisnode Polska more than PLN 940,000. The company used data from public registers but did not inform the persons (GDPR Art. 14). — [Rzeczpospolita](https://pro.rp.pl/abc-firmy/art39132691-kara-od-uodo-dla-firmy-za-przetwarzanie-danych-z-rejestrow-publicznych); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/03/30/covert-data-scraping-on-watch-as-eu-dpa-lays-down-radical-gdpr-red-line)

The NSA (Supreme Administrative Court) dismissed the cassation of the company. The NSA said that transparency is the rule and that the exception "disproportionate effort" (Art. 14(5)(b)) must be read narrowly. [VERIFY: sources differ on the exact WSA outcome] — [UODO](https://uodo.gov.pl/en/553/1572); [prawo.pl](https://www.prawo.pl/kadry/pierwsza-kara-nalozona-przez-uodo-na-bisnode-wyrok-nsa,523262.html); [bezprawnik.pl](https://bezprawnik.pl/kara-za-naruszenie-rodo-uchylona/)

- The Italian Garante published guidance on protection against mass web scraping; UODO reported it in its bulletin. — [UODO bulletin](https://nowybiuletyn.uodo.gov.pl/sztuczna-inteligencja-wskazowki-od-wloskiego-organu-nadzorczego-dotyczace-ochrony-danych-osobowych-przed-zbieraniem-danych-z-internetu/?pdf=1905)
- **DPIA**: UODO published a list of processing operations that need a DPIA (GDPR Art. 35(4)), in 2019. The EDPB criticised the first version because it was a closed list. A UODO communication of 31 March 2025 mentions automated operations and profiling. [VERIFY: I did not read the list text] — [UODO list (PDF)](https://uodo.gov.pl/pl/file/1725); [prawo.pl](https://www.prawo.pl/biznes/ocena-ryzyka-przetwrzania-danych-wykaz-czynnosci-uodo-ma-wady,325350.html); [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/pl-pl/expert-insights/ocena-dpia-dla-systemow-ai)
- The EDPB criteria for a DPIA include "evaluation or scoring", "data concerning vulnerable data subjects" (children) and "innovative use or application of new technological solutions"; two or more criteria usually mean a DPIA is necessary. [Not fetched in this session] — [WP29/EDPB Guidelines WP248 rev.01](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/items/611236)
- **Digital Omnibus (GDPR part)**: still a proposal in November 2026. The Council adopted its negotiating mandate on 21 October 2026; the Parliament had no position; trilogues had not started. The Council mandate keeps the current definition of personal data and adds a rule for pseudonymised data. [VERIFY: secondary sources] — [technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/11/10/gdpr-changes-digital-omnibus-council-position/); [eudigitalpolicy.eu](https://eudigitalpolicy.eu/instruments/data-omnibus/)

### Inferences
Design checklist (inferred, not legal advice):
1. **Legal basis.** For voluntary submissions by creators: consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) or contract. If PCI or a JST runs the radar as a statutory task: public task (Art. 6(1)(e)) with a legal basis in national law. Choose one per purpose and record it.
2. **Minors 16-17.** Consent from a 16-year-old is valid for the online service under Art. 8 in Poland. Ask for the year of birth only. Under 16: block or require parent consent. Do not publish the name of a minor by default.
3. **Separate the innovation from the person.** Score the innovation record. Keep the creator contact in a separate table with access control. Public view shows the institution and a pseudonym or "creator team" by default.
4. **Automatic detection from public pages.** Collect institution-level information (school, project, description). Do not store names of teachers or pupils from scraped pages. If you must store a person name, the Art. 14 notice duty applies (Bisnode); plan a notice and an opt-out.
5. **DPIA.** Scoring + possible data of children + new technology meets at least two EDPB criteria. Prepare a short DPIA for a real deployment. For the prototype, write a one-page DPIA outline in the documentation.
6. **Retention and rights.** Define retention periods, an export and a delete function per creator (Art. 15-17, 20).
7. **No GDPR change to rely on.** The GDPR part of the Digital Omnibus is not law in November 2026.

## C2. EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689), Digital Omnibus on AI and the Polish AI act

### Takeaway
Scoring of innovations is not in itself an Annex III high-risk use. It can become high-risk if the score decides access to education or vocational training, is used for selection of persons, or profiles natural persons. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) moved Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027. Article 50 transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026. Poland adopted its AI act (Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1003) in July 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Annex III point 3 (education and vocational training): (a) AI systems to "determine access or admission" or "assign natural persons to educational and vocational training institutions"; (b) "evaluate learning outcomes"; (c) assess "the appropriate level of education"; (d) monitor prohibited behaviour during tests. — [AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- Annex III point 4 (employment): (a) "recruitment or selection of natural persons"; (b) decisions that affect work-related relationships. — [AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- Annex III point 5(a): systems used "by public authorities or on behalf of public authorities" "to evaluate the eligibility of natural persons for essential public assistance benefits and services", and to grant, reduce, revoke or reclaim them. — [AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- Art. 6(3): an Annex III system is not high-risk if it does not pose a significant risk and it performs a narrow procedural task, improves the result of a completed human activity, detects decision-making patterns (without replacing human assessment), or performs a preparatory task. But an Annex III system "shall always be considered to be high-risk where the AI system performs profiling of natural persons". — [AI Act Art. 6](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/6/)
- Art. 6(4): a provider who considers an Annex III system not high-risk must document the assessment before placing it on the market, and is subject to registration under Art. 49(2). — [AI Act Art. 6](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/6/)
- Art. 50(1): providers must design AI systems that interact directly with natural persons so that the persons know that they interact with an AI system, unless this is obvious. — [AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Art. 50(2): providers of systems that generate synthetic text, audio, image or video must mark outputs in a machine-readable format. Art. 50(4): deployers must disclose AI-generated text published "to inform the public on matters of public interest", unless the content "has undergone a process of human review or editorial control" and a person holds editorial responsibility. Art. 50(5): information must be clear, at the latest at first interaction, and must meet accessibility requirements. Application date: 2 August 2026. — [AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- **Digital Omnibus on AI**: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026; entry into force 27 July 2026. Annex III high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027 (before: 2 August 2026). Annex I (product) high-risk obligations apply from 2 August 2028. Generative systems on the market before 2 August 2026 must meet Art. 50(2) by 2 December 2026. — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Digital Omnibus](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-act-explorer/digital-omnibus/); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Parliament endorsed the Omnibus on 16 June 2026; the Council approved it on 29 June 2026. [VERIFY: secondary] — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-the-new-high-risk-ai-deadlines-after-council-approval)
- Art. 4 (AI literacy, applicable since 2 February 2025) changed with the Omnibus: providers and deployers must "take measures to support the development of AI literacy"; there is no required level for each person. — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Digital Omnibus](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-act-explorer/digital-omnibus/)
- The Omnibus gives SMEs and small mid-caps simplified technical documentation and priority access to EU-level sandboxes. — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Digital Omnibus](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-act-explorer/digital-omnibus/)
- **Polish AI act ("ustawa o systemach sztucznej inteligencji")**: Sejm passed it on 11 June 2026 (421 for, 3 against, 18 abstained). The Senate adopted 25 amendments on 25 June 2026. The act is dated 3 July 2026, published in Dziennik Ustaw on 27 July 2026 as item 1003. It entered into force on 11 August 2026; Art. 8-18 and chapters 3-5, 8 and 9 apply from 28 October 2026. It creates a new commission for AI development and safety with a social advisory council, and regulatory sandboxes free of charge for micro, small and medium enterprises. [VERIFY: dates from search summary; confirm in the act] — [Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1003 (ELI)](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2026/1003); [act text (PDF)](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026/1003/text.pdf); [RMF24](https://www.rmf24.pl/fakty/polska/news-co-dalej-z-ai-w-polsce-ustawa-o-sztucznej-inteligencji-przyj,nId,8089990); [Bankier](https://www.bankier.pl/wiadomosc/Polska-wdraza-AI-Act-Powstal-nowy-organ-nadzoru-nad-sztuczna-inteligencja-9149707.html); [Ministerstwo Cyfryzacji](https://www.gov.pl/web/cyfryzacja/koniec-ery-nieuchwytnych-algorytmow--projekt-ustawy-o-systemach-sztucznej-inteligencji-przyjety-przez-rade-ministrow)

### Inferences
Design checklist (inferred, not legal advice):
1. **Score the innovation, not the person.** Do not compute features about the creator (age, school grades, personality). This keeps the system out of "profiling of natural persons", which removes the Art. 6(3) exit.
2. **Human decision.** Present the score as a "preparatory task" for a PCI analyst. The analyst confirms the path and the match. Record this in the UI and in the docs (Art. 6(3)(d)).
3. **Risk triggers to avoid.** Do not use the score alone to admit young creators into PCI programmes, scholarships or training (Annex III 3(a)), or to select persons for jobs (Annex III 4(a)). If PCI wants this later, the system becomes high-risk from 2 December 2027.
4. **Document the Art. 6(3) assessment** in the README (one paragraph), so the jury sees that the team checked it.
5. **Art. 50 transparency (in force).** If an LLM writes summaries, recommendations or chat answers: label them "wygenerowane przez AI", keep a human review step before publication, and mark generated text in the data model.
6. **AI literacy (Art. 4).** Add a short "how the score works and its limits" page for PCI staff.

## C3. Copyright, text and data mining (TDM), database right and open data

### Takeaway
Poland implemented the DSM Directive TDM exceptions in an act of 26 July 2024 (in force 20 September 2024). Automated analysis of published works is allowed unless the rightholder reserves it in a machine-readable form. The open data act of 2021 governs re-use of public-sector information. Respect robots.txt and other machine-readable opt-outs, keep copies only for the TDM purpose, and prefer links and short extracts over copies of full texts.

### Cited Findings
- Act of 26 July 2024 amending the copyright act ("ustawa o zmianie ustawy o prawie autorskim i prawach pokrewnych"), Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1254, published 19 August 2024, in force 20 September 2024 (one month after publication). Only Art. 1 pkt 24 (Art. 86¹) has a later date (20 February 2025). — [ELI, Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1254](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2024/1254); [act text](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2024/1254/text.html)

- General TDM exception, Art. 26³ ust. 1: "Wolno zwielokrotniać rozpowszechnione utwory w celu eksploracji tekstów i danych, chyba że uprawniony zastrzegł inaczej." Ust. 2: for works available online, the reservation must be "w formacie przeznaczonym do odczytu maszynowego" with metadata. ## C4. EUPL-1.2 obligations

### Takeaway
EUPL-1.2 is a copyleft licence that also covers network use ("providing access to its essential functionalities"). It has a compatibility clause and an appendix of compatible licences (GPL, AGPL, LGPL, MPL-2.0, EPL, OSL, CeCILL, LiLiQ, CC BY-SA 3.0 for non-software). Permissive dependencies (MIT, BSD, Apache-2.0) are normally usable. Keep all notices, add the licence file and a source link.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 5 obligations of the licensee: keep intact all copyright, patent and trademark notices; include a copy of the notices and of the licence with each copy; mark modified derivative works with the modification and its date. — [Interoperable Europe, EUPL 1.2](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl)
- Copyleft: distribution of the work or derivative works must be under EUPL (or a later version, unless "EUPL v. 1.2 only"). The licensee "cannot offer or impose any additional terms or conditions". — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl)
- Compatibility: a derivative work that combines the work with a work under a compatible licence may be distributed under that compatible licence; in a conflict, "the obligations of the Compatible Licence shall prevail". — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl); [EUPL compatible licences](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/page/eupl-compatible-open-source-licences)
- Appendix (compatible licences): GPL v2 and v3, AGPL v3, OSL v2.1 and v3.0, EPL v1.0, CeCILL v2.0 and v2.1, MPL v2, LGPL v2.1 and v3, CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported (for works other than software), EUPL v1.1 and v1.2, LiLiQ-R and LiLiQ-R+. — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl)
- Source code: when the licensee distributes copies, it must give a machine-readable copy of the source code or a link to a repository where the source is easily and freely available. — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl)
- Art. 1: "Distribution or Communication" includes "providing access to its essential functionalities" (network/SaaS use). — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl)
- Art. 2: the licensor grants royalty-free, non-exclusive rights to its patents, as far as necessary to use the licensed rights. — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl)
- Legal protection: no right to use trade names, trademarks or names of the licensor, except to describe the origin of the work. — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl)
- Law and court: the law of the EU Member State of the licensor applies; disputes go to the court where the licensor resides (CJEU if the licensor is an EU institution). — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl)
- How to apply: put "Licensed under the EUPL" after the copyright notice. SPDX identifier: `EUPL-1.2`. — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/licence/european-union-public-licence-version-12-eupl); [SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/EUPL-1.2)
- Niklas Plutte calls the EUPL an "interoperable copyleft licence". [Opinion] — [Wikipedia, EUPL](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUPL)

### Inferences
Checklist for the repository (inferred):
1. Add `LICENSE` with the full EUPL-1.2 text (EN; the PL version is also official) and an SPDX header `SPDX-License-Identifier: EUPL-1.2` in source files.
2. Add a third-party notices file: each dependency, its licence and copyright notice. Apache-2.0 dependencies need their NOTICE text kept.
3. Avoid dependencies with licences that are not compatible with EUPL and not permissive (for example SSPL, proprietary SDKs, "non-commercial" model weights). Check the licence of each AI model and dataset.
4. Do not put the PCI or Ministry logo into the code as a trademark grant; the EUPL gives no trademark rights.
5. The patent grant covers only patents of the contributors. The team must not have patent claims it wants to keep.
6. Contributions of a minor (if a young PCI creator contributes code) need a clear right to licence; see C1 Gaps.

## C5. Public-sector deployment: accessibility, KRI, cybersecurity (KSC/NIS2), hosting

### Takeaway
A public body that deploys the radar must meet the digital accessibility act (WCAG 2.1 AA via EN 301 549, accessibility statement), the National Interoperability Framework (KRI, regulation of 21 May 2024) and, if the operator is a key or important entity, the amended KSC act (NIS2) in force since April 2026. Build to WCAG 2.1 AA from the start, use open formats and an API, and plan EU hosting.

### Inferences
Design checklist (inferred, not legal advice):
1. **Accessibility.** Semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, contrast 4.5:1, alt text, labels on form fields, no information only by colour, text alternatives for charts. Add a draft "deklaracja dostępności" page. Test with an automatic checker (for example axe or WAVE) before the demo.
2. **Interoperability (KRI).** Open formats (JSON, CSV), a documented REST API, UTF-8, export of all records. This also supports transferability to other regions.
3. **Security.** No login for public reading; strong authentication for PCI staff; role-based access; audit log of score changes; secrets only in environment variables; backups. If a JST or PCI is a NIS2 entity, the system is in scope of its risk management.
4. **Hosting.** Plan EU hosting so that no personal data leaves the EEA (GDPR Chapter V). If an external LLM API is used, send no personal data, or use an EU region with a data processing agreement.
5. **Plain Polish UI.** WCAG and the target group (16-30) both favour short, simple text.
